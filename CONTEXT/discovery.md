# Discovery — the questions research cannot answer

Phase 3 of `CONTEXT/workflow.md`. You arrive here with research notes already on paper. Do NOT re-ask things research already told you. Lead with what you found, then ask only the gaps below.

The questions are grouped by what they decide downstream. Skip any group that does not apply to this engagement.

---

## Confirm research

Before asking new questions, read your research findings back to the user in 5 to 8 short bullets:

- What they currently sell (one sentence).
- Who appears to buy from them.
- The three direct competitors you found.
- The sector reflex you intend to AVOID.
- The voice quotes you pulled from their own copy.
- The current site's main problem (if they have one).

Ask: "Anything wrong here?" Let them correct you before going further.

---

## Group A — Positioning + voice (drives copy, headlines, tone)

These shape every word that ships. Skip none.

1. **What do you stand for, and what do you refuse to do?** The refusal is as load-bearing as the promise.
2. **Tagline and promise.** Do they have one? If not, draft three options and let them react.
3. **Voice axes.** Where do they sit on each: Warm vs Cool. Playful vs Serious. Plain vs Technical. Understated vs Bold. Get a position on each, not a vibe.
4. **A brand they admire** (and one they can't stand). Ask why. The contrast tells you more than positive examples alone.
5. **Words they love. Words they ban.** The defaults (no em dashes, no AI tells, no exclamation marks) carry from the toolkit. Add the client's own.
6. **Three real proof points.** Numbers, named clients (with permission), outcomes. If they don't have proof yet, the site leans on positioning, not stats. Confirm which case applies.

## Group B — Visual direction (drives design system)

These set tokens. Decisions, not adjectives.

7. **Colour.** Existing brand colours? Hex or Pantone. If not, the mood: warm or cool, saturated or muted, high contrast or quiet.
8. **Light or dark?** Do not default. Ask them to describe in one sentence who views the site, where, in what ambient light, in what mood. Let that sentence force the answer.
9. **Type personality.** Geometric, humanist, editorial-serif, mono-technical, grotesque. One font or a display + body pairing. Anchor to a brand they showed you in Q4.
10. **Motion level.** Energetic, balanced, restrained, still. Ask for a physical metaphor if they have one.
11. **Logo + assets.** What exists. What's usable. What needs to be remade or commissioned.

## Group C — Structure + content (drives the page set)

These cut or keep pages.

12. **What does the visitor need to believe to act?** Reverse-engineer the page set from that, not from "every site has an About page."
13. **Services or offers.** The real list in priority order. One-line "what it is" plus the outcome it buys.
14. **Proof and work.** Real case studies, logos, testimonials. What can ship with permission and real detail?
15. **Process.** How they actually work with a client, in phases.
16. **Engagement models and pricing.** Project, retainer, productised. Are prices public?
17. **The one action.** The single most important thing a visitor does. Everything points at it.
18. **Insights or content engine.** Publishing now? If yes, seed three real angles. If no, leave the route out and revisit later.

## Group D — Logistics (drives launch + handoff)

The boring bits that block ship if missed.

19. **Domain + registrar.** Owned, pointing where now, who controls DNS.
20. **Integrations needed.** Newsletter, CRM, booking, analytics. Which exist, which to set up.
21. **Inbox for form submissions.** A real monitored address.
22. **Legal.** Company name, registered address, privacy basis, accessibility statement.
23. **Timeline + sign-off.** When does it ship? Who is the named approver?
24. **Budget posture.** Not the number unless they offer it; a posture (lean, comfortable, premium). It changes how aggressive the build can be.

---

## After discovery

Turn answers + research into `CONTEXT/brand-brief.md` (copy from `brand-brief.template.md`). Take the user through it section by section. Get explicit approval at the brief gate before any code.
